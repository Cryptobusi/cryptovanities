## Block — Site Status (9 October 2026)

Egonomic Anonymous is a conceptual economic framework and interactive website by @trancesage. It is not a cryptocurrency, a new mint, or a finished protocol. It is a public bookkeeping and visibility rule set, plus browser-based models, that treats the individual as a “third seat” alongside the state and the firm.

### Central thesis
> “They printed the money. I am keeping the book.”  
> Same rails. Anyone may issue. No one may hide the print.  
> If it cannot be shown, it is not policy.  
> Sovereignty is the refusal.

The project argues that states can expand the monetary base and firms can issue credit that clears like cash, while losses and second prints often stay off public books. The proposed fix is not to abolish either seat, but to put a published order in front of both of them and give the person the same right (and duty) to read and refuse lines that skip that order.

### The three seats
- **First seat (State)**: Can tax and, in a crisis, expand the base. Under the rule it must follow a posted minting schedule; a mint not on the tape is not a mint.
- **Second seat (Firm)**: Can issue equity and debt against a limited shell. Losses stay on the book; commercial paper does not clear at par with legal tender; discount and maturity are named.
- **Third seat (Person)**: Reads the tape, pays the basket first, and refuses a print, price, or profit that skipped the order. Bound by the same rules—no quieter standard for the individual either.

The person is not a new government or a new bank. The third seat is the right to keep a public record and the duty to follow the same restraints.

### The order (the stack)
The live sequence on the site is:

**Floor → Mint → Issue → Tape → Charge → Agent → Thin → Desert**

(Some materials also mention a ninth step, Retire.)

1. **Floor** — A small basket of living costs (example opening figure around $1,470) plus a small income is paid first from surplus that has already been named. Survival is not priced at the door and is not residual after fiscal fights or eligibility checks.
2. **Mint** — New units enter only on a public schedule. No private window. A second print of the same unit fails in public. Seigniorage is a listed price.
3. **Issue** — Households, firms, and the treasury may name credit on the same rail. That paper is not money at the register; it does not clear at par.
4. **Tape** — Five things must be shown: who holds the claim (title), what was minted, what lien sits on it, how it converted, and what an agent did. Private life stays off the public tape. An unpublished act is a story, not policy.
5. **Charge** — Discount and risk charge are two named numbers taken before residual title. No friend rates.
6. **Agent** — Software is a clerk of the posted rules. It pays the floor on the clock, flags side prices, and refuses a second print. It does not mint and does not decide who deserves the basket.
7. **Thin** — A quiet or thin book stays wide. Do not invent a tight price when the board is empty. Width is information.
8. **Desert** — What remains after the floor and the listed prices is titled to the issuer (including zero). Confiscating it does not make the books fair. A small desert can be titled toward the next floor.

The whitepaper condenses related ideas into five layers (Floor, Mint, Issue, Tape, Agents), while the live site works with the longer eight-step order.

### Key operating principles
- Same rules and same facilities bind state, firm, and person. None may write a rule it does not also live under.
- Claims arise from recorded exchange, not by declaration.
- Settlement is civil and in equity; force is what remains when the ledger is refused, not the way the ledger is written.
- A missing line is not a privilege.
- The floor is senior and cannot be raided to service old debt.
- Hedera is treated as a way to prove a write; it does not write the law. Writes are cheap (~$0.001 counted in $Trust); reading the tape is free.

### What exists on the site right now
The domain is a collection of live pages and interactive browser models (not full on-chain applications that hold keys or take custody):

- **Notes / bound order** — Chapter-style pages covering door, stack against fiat, floor, mint & issue, window lock, tape & agent, thin & desert, desk trails, plus a worked invoice example.
- **Third Seat** — Front door for the framework; how to start with an existing room that already collects dues, post three numbers (basket, fee, remainder), keep the tape limited, and only write on-chain when a line must be shown.
- **Floor desk, Market, Enter/Walk the invoice, Catalog, Sealroom** — Interactive models of the steps. Everything stays in the browser until a user chooses to sign with their own HashPack wallet.
- **Whitepaper, refusal, granted, line, stack, EZego (plain words), report, papers** — Essays and longer readings.
- **Contribution path** — Optional gift from an X wallet to the X wallet @trancesage (described as half floor / half development). The page itself does not hold keys or take the transfer.
- Mentions of HashPack, $Trust, ClubHbar.ℏ (example account 0.0.527206), and related tools.

A working-book PDF (“A working book for the third seat,” dated October 2026) and other papers are linked. The project is marked “#LeGoMiEgo in Beta.” Recent X activity from the author continues the same themes (sovereignty as refusal, public tape, third seat) rather than announcing a specific product launch or roadmap.

### Practical starting points (as of 9 October 2026)
- Read the Notes page and the Third Seat page first.
- Try the interactive desks to see how the order is modeled.
- Follow @trancesage for ongoing posts.
- If you want to support the floor, the site describes the X-wallet gift method.

In short, it is an attempt to make the monetary and credit system’s book public and ordered—floor first, no hidden prints, same rules for all three seats—while leaving the actual state and firm in place and treating the person as the reader and refuser of skipped lines.
